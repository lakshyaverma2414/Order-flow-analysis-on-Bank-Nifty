# Conclusions

The central hypothesis tested in this repository is that reconstructed option order flow contains predictive information about future premium changes **after** controlling for spot movement. The empirical evidence does **not** support that hypothesis in the current dataset.

Across the full sample, the mean residual correlation is effectively zero. Across clustering exercises, no stable market regime exhibits economically meaningful positive residual correlation. The negative result survives methodological refinement from V1 to V2, which strengthens confidence that the conclusion is not an artifact of the clustering design.

The most defensible interpretation is that option flow in this sample behaves as a **reactive** market variable. It reflects hedging pressure, post-move participation, and liquidity adjustment, but it does not consistently lead future residual premium changes. This is a useful empirical result in its own right: it narrows the space of plausible microstructure hypotheses and makes the boundary between descriptive flow measures and predictive signals much clearer.
